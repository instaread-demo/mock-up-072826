Happy birthday Liddy: Salisbury’s senator has had always heart for city

By Karen Kistler

On the eve of her 90th birthday, Senator Elizabeth Dole, who was born and raised in Salisbury, is remembered by many who call the city their home. While Dole doesn’t live in the Salisbury area, memories and the love for Salisbury are still in her heart.

“Her heart is a big part of Salisbury,” said Gia Colombraro executive assistant and scheduler to Senator Elizabeth Dole, “and Salisbury is a big part of her heart.”

Colombraro, who has been with Dole for 28 years, shared that Dole has through the years and still today called Salisbury her Rock of Gibraltar and her goal of making a positive difference in the lives of others came from the values instilled in her from those early years and the people from Salisbury.

The daughter of John Van Hanford and Mary Ella Cathey Hanford, Dole will be celebrating her 90th birthday July 29. Colombraro shared that Dole’s mother lived to be about 102 and her grandmother Cathey lived to be about 100.

When asked what she would consider her greatest accomplishments, Colombraro said she thought it would be trying to make life better, which she has done and noted one of those ways was safety, particularly during her time with the Department of Transportation.

The team, she noted, was able to accomplish the enforcement of the use of seatbelts, having air bags in cars and changing the legal drinking age to 21 when she served as Secretary of Transportation under the Reagan administration.

The combination of those, she said, has resulted in saving many lives, and “I would say that is a very important part of her life.”

Dole’s work with the Red Cross is another, having served as president and completely restructuring the way they tag, collect and distribute the blood supply. She was there eight years and loved working there and is a very compassionate person. “Her work in the United States and with the International Red Cross was very compelling for her,” said Colombraro.

Dole continues to make a difference at the Elizabeth Dole Foundation, which she founded in 2012 when her husband the late Senator Bob Dole was in the hospital for months at Walter Reed. While spending those months there, she recognized the need of caregivers of wounded warriors, she said, seeing the challenges, which Colombraro said, led to “develop and found the Elizabeth Dole Foundation.”

However, the difference Dole has made in others’ lives hasn’t been limited to her career, but in many quiet ways remembering those who impacted her life, and as her friend Margaret Kluttz said, she has “stayed in touch with her hometown in personal ways.”

She recalled how Dole gave her opportunities she would not have had, including reaching out to her to serve as her state director of her offices in North Carolina when she was in the Senate.

“We’ve just remained friends outside of that in just such touching ways,” said Kluttz, noting that Dole while working for one of the presidents would make time and call her mother every day, noting her thoughtfulness in both incidents.

Remembering and helping others back home, Colombraro said Dole sponsored dozens of seats at the Norvell Theater when it opened years ago and there are plaques on those seats in memory and honor of people who have influenced her life.

She shared how during COVID when people were secluded, the Doles had an everyday goal of calling as many people as they could to see how they were doing, noting that still to this day it’s making a difference for many who remember that gesture.

Kluttz remembered how Dole wanted to do something tangible for the Nazareth orphanage, something more than just giving money as she wanted to find out what the children there loved to do and something special they might not be able to ever do. They learned the children wanted to take a ride in a limousine and Dole wanted to make that happen and said, “why don’t we find a way for them to have a limousine ride around Salisbury and ice cream or go to the movies or something.”

Quietly doing things behind the scenes was not uncommon for Dole as Kluttz remembers her having people over for dinner when she was in Salisbury after her father died. It would be a holiday and they reached out to families and host those that perhaps didn’t have a big celebration for Thanksgiving or Christmas or just a weekend.

“It would be somebody that you wouldn’t even think she would have that kind of outreach to,” she added. “It was very quiet but meaningful.

And Dole would go to the nursing home and visit her parents’ friends or the parents of her own friends, not in an official capacity, said Kluttz, but just as an act of kindness.

“It was such a powerful thing for me to become acquainted with someone doing all the things on a different stage from just Salisbury but never forgetting Salisbury that really had such a tremendous influence on me to just realize that people do get their wings to fly here but they don’t ever really fly away,” said Kluttz.

Colombraro shared how Dole’s faith is so important to her and has a quiet time each day spending at least 30 minutes reading her Bible or watching an old Billy Graham crusade, noting she and the Grahams were good friends.

An excellent student, Dole always studied, said Colombraro and said her mother instilled in her that “if you’re done with your homework, how about entering that essay contest?” There was always a little bit more work to be done, something that she noted carried her her through the years to achieve so much.

Dole, who went to Duke, studied at Oxford for a summer and then went to Harvard, one of only 24 women in a class of 550, is described as a risk taker, as Colombraro said Dole was not afraid to try something new or explore new places and loved traveling to North Carolina and representing the state and military bases in North Carolina hold a special place in her heart.

Animals have always been an important part of Dole’s life. The couple wanted to get a dog, and she decided to gift Senator Bob with one when he became majority leader, and surprised him with a sign that said, Leader, during a press conference. She now has Leader IV, another miniature Schnauzer.

She also recalled a story she had heard of when Dole was young and her grandmother came to visit and saw her pushing an ant or some sort of bug under the rug, and asked what she was doing.

Dole’s reply was, “don’t tell mother, she’ll kill it.”

When asked what advice she might share with the next generation, Colombraro said she thought it would be to follow Dole’s goal for her own life and that of making a positive difference in the lives of others, not be afraid to take risks, and step outside the box and try things, as she shared how Dole “was the first to do so many things, particularly as a woman all those years” and to travel, something Colombraro said Dole did, going to the Soviet Union in the late 50s wanting to travel and explore and to the Orient when she was young.

She is a “tremendous blessing in my life,” she said of Dole, “and I know she is a blessing in so many others.

“She has definitely always tried to make a difference in people’s lives, and she has,” she said, “and Senator Bob was like that too. They were a perfect fit in that way.”
