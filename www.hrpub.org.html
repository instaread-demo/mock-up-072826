Developing Midwives' Educational Communication Competence in Delivering Adolescent Reproductive Health Care


Dini Saraswati Handayani 1,*, Ace Suryadi 2, Dinn Wahyudin 2, Encep Syarief Nurdin 2, Anita Megawati Pajrin 3
1 Master Program in Midwifery, Department of Public Health, Faculty of Medicine, Universitas Padjadjaran, Indonesia
2 General Education and Character Program, Universitas Pendidikan Indonesia, Indonesia
3 Midwifery Program, Politeknik Kementrian Kesehatan Bandung, Indonesia

ABSTRACT

Educational communication competence is essential in midwifery practice, particularly for adolescent reproductive health education, as it encompasses the ability to communicate health information clearly, empathetically, and in culturally appropriate and participatory ways. Despite its importance, many midwives continue to experience difficulties in applying these skills effectively. This study aimed to design and evaluate the INSPIRE training model (Identify, Navigate, Strategize, Practice, Integrate, Reflect, Evaluate), a structured framework intended to strengthen communication competence through reflective and practice-based learning. An exploratory sequential mixed-method sociocultural approach was employed, beginning with qualitative interviews involving 15 midwives across three provinces in Java to identify needs and contextual barriers. The qualitative findings revealed five major themes: challenges in simplifying information, limited interpersonal communication strategies, lack of access to formal training, cultural and social norm constraints, and expectations for sustainable competency strengthening. The findings guided the development of a training module, which was subsequently tested with 60 midwives in primary healthcare services. Evaluation using pre- and post-tests demonstrated a significant improvement in competence, with mean scores rising from 59.23 to 89.77 and an average N-Gain of 0.75, categorized as high effectiveness. These findings highlight the potential of the INSPIRE model to provide a contextually relevant and scalable approach for enhancing midwives' educational communication competence, thereby contributing to improved adolescent reproductive health services and supporting broader health system transformation.

KEYWORDS
Educational Communication, Midwives, Adolescent Reproductive Health, Training, INSPIRE Model
